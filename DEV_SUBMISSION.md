## Inspiration

When DEV said *comfort food*, I didn't think of restaurant platters or Sunday lunch.

I thought of home. College. Friends walking through the city with no plan except **to find something to eat**. I thought of the **aroma** that hits you when you pass a **thela** — that small street cart — and your feet turn toward it before your brain catches up.

That smell. That steam. That queue. That's where this project starts.

### Chai — the anytime drink

Back here in India, **chai is not a morning-only drink**. It never was.

Wake up early? Chai.

Study? Chai.

Work? Chai.

Friends hanging out? Chai.

Just woke up from a nap? **First thing — chai.**

Cutting, full, adrak wali, elaichi — the variations change, but the feeling doesn't. One hot sip and you feel **totally alive and awake**. That's not marketing copy. That's memory.

Back home, it's tradition. When a guest visits — any programme, any festival, any gathering — the **first thing** offered is chai. It sounds odd to outsiders. Here it's normal. It's just how things work.

I come from a town built around agriculture and education. Chai isn't one crowd's drink. Workers, teachers, officers, employees — everyone has their spot. There's a chai **thela wala** near us who keeps a **separate monthly account** for each regular. They settle up at month-end. It sounds extreme until you live it — and it isn't addiction. Chai is the fastest, easiest drink with a little belly-filling warmth. People pair it with biscuits or toast. Folks in agriculture often eat **salted rotis with chai**. My father leaves early for work — chai then too. I used to have mine with biscuits and toast. Same ritual, different schedules.

### With friends — the real comfort menu

When we gathered for a walk or to kill free time, we didn't just walk. We **ate our way through the city**. Sometimes the whole evening was one food stop after another.

**Pani puri** was — and still is — my absolute go-to. Tangy, semi-spicy, that one-bite explosion where teekha pani, potato, and crunch land together. You can't eat it slow. You shouldn't. Six puris, six little decisions, zero regrets.

And here's the tradition where I'm from: a true pani puri lover always asks for **two suki papdi** after the last puri. If you don't — well, don't call yourself a lover. It's the finishing move here — crunchy and dry after all that pani. Non-negotiable.

**Vada pav** is a whole culture. There are *tons* of famous shops for one dish. Same ingredients everywhere — pav, batata vada, chutneys — and yet **every shop tastes different**. We picked the stall based on **mood**. Feeling classic? One place. Feeling spicy? Another. Feeling nostalgic? The one from college days.

A couple recently opened a shop nearby with **35+ variations** — genuinely fantastic, mind-blowing. That place changed the game, but mood-based shop picking never stopped.

I still prefer **Mumbai style**: green chutney, onion, vada, dry chutney, tamarind chutney. Simple layers — wholesome ingredients, nothing fancy, just done right.

And honestly? **One vada pav is enough** for an average appetite. Fast, filling, done. That's the beauty of it.

**Banana chips** are the road home.

I belong to **Jalgaon, Maharashtra** — Banana City. It sits in the heart of India's banana belt: the prime hub of banana production in the country. Drive those highways and you don't only see plantations. Every few kilometres there are **small shops** frying **banana chips live** — paper-thin nendran coins, still hot from the oil, tossed with **black salt** and **red chilli powder**.

You stop. You take a paper cone. You pinch a coin with every sip of chai. Fresh. Loud. Crisp. That smell of hot oil and spice is as much home as the thela itself — and it's the plate on this counter.

Our breakfast-with-friends wasn't always idli or dosa. Sometimes it was **poha** — and never plain:

- **Rasa poha**
- **Misal poha**
- **Tari poha**
- A squeeze of **lemon** on top
- A pinch of **black salt**

Wah. Even writing this makes my mouth water.

These were my **go-to comfort foods with friends** in college and the years we were still learning who we were. Even today, when we gather, we **don't miss the chance**. Same hunger. Same lanes. Same laughter. Different year.

### At home — mother's kitchen

At home, my mother often made **idli**, **dosa**, **sambar**, and **poha**.

**Poha** became *the* comfort dish when I was hungry **between lunch and dinner** — that in-between hunger when you don't want a full meal but need warmth. Amma would make it quickly, and somehow it always tasted like she knew exactly how hungry you were.

**Idli and dosa** weren't snacks for me — I often replaced a full meal with them. Nutrient-rich, satisfying, served with **sambar**, **coconut chutney**, **potato sabji** spread inside, and a thin layer of **dry chutney** on top. Still the best combination for me.

The dosa I love most? **Paper-thin and super crisp** — the kind that crackles when you bite — with **cheese** melted inside and **coconut chutney** on the side. (The counter art is paneer dosa — same love, different stuffing — but the feeling is identical.)

### Why I built Nukkad Chat Corner

All of this lives at a **thela** — not a fancy restaurant, not a food court. Steel counter. String lights. Queue on the wall. Someone calling out order numbers. The smell of chai and fried batter before you even see the menu.

That's the world I wanted to rebuild in CSS.

**Nukkad Chat Corner** is the name of the website — a single-page night **thela** of eight dishes from my real life, sculpted in **pure CSS**. No food photographs. No menu prices. No frameworks. Just gradients, `@keyframes`, `@property`, `:has()` rituals, steam, and a little JavaScript where CSS alone couldn't finish the story.

Each plate carries a **ritual line** (how we actually eat it). Click opens a comfort-plate theater — enlarged CSS sculpture first, then story, craft tags, and optional nutrition tucked below.

## Journey

### Why a thela, not a gallery?

Most CSS art sits on a white page like a museum piece. Comfort food in India doesn't live in museums. It lives at a **thela** — steel surface, string lights, queue on the wall, chai steam in the air.

I didn't want a menu website. I wanted you to feel like you're **walking past a shop at night** and stopping because the smell won.

The micro welcome curtain came first — kulhad steam, *"Arre bhai, cutting chai garam hai…"* — then a brand-forward hero chai and a plates theater with signature food rituals, not ₹ price tags.

### CSS is the star

Every dish is built from scratch:

- **`repeating-linear-gradient`** — pav crust, kulhad ridges, wood grain, banana leaf ribs
- **`radial-gradient` / `conic-gradient`** — tea meniscus, puri shells, idli pores, dosa griddle burns
- **`@keyframes`** — steam convection, string-light flicker, heat shimmer, lamp pool pulse
- **`transform-style: preserve-3d`** — pani puri isometric clusters, idli platters, dosa cones
- **`:has()` food hovers** — hover the chai *cup*, not the card, and the steam responds

Cards stay **static** so scrolling stays smooth. The food inside moves. Early versions lifted whole cards and the page fought itself — the final build keeps cards still and lets the food breathe.

### Light JavaScript, on purpose

**Indori Poha** is a CSS flake mound — box-shadow rice, radial turmeric, a CSS spoon. Everything on the counter is CSS.

Near-zero JS: one tiny `app.js` only wires reduced-motion and keyboard Escape/Enter for the plate radios. Welcome, back-to-top, lights, scroll, and the plate overlay are CSS/HTML. No framework. No animation loop. JavaScript supports the story; **CSS tells it**.

### Lighting and atmosphere

Warm string bulbs. Amber corner pools. Neon spill. Chai steam catching lamplight. The kind of night you know from a **galli** when you're waiting for your order with friends.

`prefers-reduced-motion` is respected throughout — welcome curtain hides, smooth scroll turns off, rituals settle.

### What I'm proud of

- The **chai glass** — because chai isn't just a beverage here, it's a lifestyle
- **Pani puri** as a 7-puri cluster — the dish I was most excited to render in CSS
- **Jalgaon banana chips** — the highway shops of home, fried live, black salt and chilli
- **Modal dish tickets** — history, how to eat, healthier style, nutrition per serving
- A counter that scrolls smooth **and** feels like the shops we grew up around
